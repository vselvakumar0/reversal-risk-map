Reversal Risk Map

Where is carbon stored in forests and mangroves most likely to be lost again? Each state or province is scored on four natural and human hazards, using open satellite and climate data. Carbon projects are an optional layer, so a region's risk can be compared with the buffer a project sets aside.

Demo data: these scores are placeholders, not measurements. The values on this map were generated arbitrarily so the site can be previewed. Run the pipeline in the repository to replace them with real results.

The buffer is the share of a project's credits held back in the registry's pooled reserve to cover losses. "Buffer low for the risk" appears when a project ranks at least two places higher on regional risk than on buffer size among the projects listed here. Project locations are approximate points and each entry needs checking against its registry.

How the score is built

Fire history

Share of the region's land that burns in an average year, 2001 to 2023.

MODIS MCD64A1 burned area, 500 m
Drought trend

How fast the region has been drying: the slope of the annual Palmer Drought Severity Index, 2001 to 2023. Wetter trends score low.

TerraClimate, about 4 km
Cyclone exposure

Hurricane-force storm track points passing within 100 km of the region per year since 1980, per 100,000 km².

NOAA IBTrACS v4
Deforestation pressure

Share of the region's year-2000 forest lost between 2014 and 2023.

Hansen Global Forest Change, 30 m

Each indicator is turned into a 0 to 100 score by ranking the region against all others (100 is the most exposed region). The overall score is the weighted average of the four, using the weights set above. It is a relative ranking, not a probability of loss.

Limits

  • Regions are states and provinces. Their size varies a lot between countries, and risk still varies inside large ones, so a regional score can differ from the risk at a given project site.
  • Forest loss in the Hansen data includes fire and plantation harvest, not only deforestation, so it overlaps with the fire indicator.
  • Past hazard is used as a guide to future hazard. Pests, disease, sea-level rise, land tenure and political risk are not covered.
  • Buffer contributions also reflect project-level factors such as management and finances, so a mismatch is a prompt for questions, not a verdict.
Boundaries: Natural Earth states and provinces, simplified (public domain). Map library: Leaflet. Code and data pipeline are in the repository.
